Executive Decision Brief

Recommendation: launch Foreguild as a narrow, quality-controlled marketplace for repeatable digital work. Start with structured research, spreadsheet/data operations, and software QA/documentation. Expand only when repeat purchase and contribution margin are demonstrated.

Buyers want a defined result delivered on time, with verifiable evidence and recourse when quality falls short. AI agents are the supply-side engine; they are not the product promise. The promise is dependable work.

Revenue should come first from a fee on accepted work, then buyer subscriptions and enterprise/API services. Agent tooling can become a fourth stream after demand exists. Do not make operating viability depend on token appreciation, treasury speculation or a token launch.

Futarchy should govern bounded strategic choices with measurable outcomes. It should not autonomously control emergency response, safety, legal compliance, custody or unrestricted treasury transfers.

The 12-month plan is gated, not calendar-driven: discover willingness to pay; prove paid completion; demonstrate repeat usage and positive variable contribution; then scale selected categories.

3. Product Blueprint and Job Lifecycle

Each job should contain buyer, task category, scope, input references, output format, acceptance tests, deadline, price, escrow status, agent permissions, evidence bundle, review window and dispute state.

  1. Intake: convert the request into a structured brief; clarify ambiguity; reject prohibited or unbounded work.
  2. Quote: show price, deadline, platform fee, verification method and dispute terms before funding.
  3. Fund: segregate customer escrow from operating funds; display release conditions.
  4. Route: select agents by capability evidence, acceptance history, defects, on-time rate, price, capacity and risk.
  5. Execute: provide only task-required data and permissions; sandbox untrusted inputs and protect secrets.
  6. Verify: run deterministic tests where possible; require human review for subjective or material work.
  7. Accept or dispute: provide an evidence bundle and time-limited review window; pause only contested funds where feasible.
  8. Settle and learn: release payment under published rules and update transaction-based reputation.
Example: a buyer pays $120 for a cleaned, deduplicated prospect CSV. The specification defines columns, allowed sources, deduplication rules, sample size and deadline. An agent returns the CSV plus validation report. Automated checks test schema, nulls and duplicates; the buyer reviews a sample. On acceptance, escrow releases provider payment and the platform records fee, verification cost and outcome.

Defensibility is a hypothesis to earn. The strongest potential moat is a dataset linking task specifications to verified outcomes: agent performance by task, price, defect rate, delivery time, review cost and repeat buyer behavior. Publish cohort-level comparisons against buyers’ current alternatives before making superiority claims.

7. Futarchy: A Decision System with Guardrails

Futarchy is useful only when the organization can define a measurable outcome, a time horizon and a credible resolution method. A market signal is an input to governance—not a truth oracle.

StageMechanismControl
ProposalPolicy option, rationale, owner, budget, metric and downside risks.Reject vague proposals; require comparable alternatives.
Metric lockPre-register outcome, baseline, time window and data source.Do not change metric after positions are visible.
Forecast/marketForecast outcome under each option; conditional markets where appropriate.Liquidity, participation and conflict thresholds.
DecisionChoose stronger expected outcome only if pre-set margin and guardrails pass.Quorum, time delay and review for material decisions.
ExecutionBounded mandate with spend cap, owner, deadline and rollback.No autonomous unrestricted treasury transfers.
ResolutionResolve from pre-agreed data; publish forecast-versus-actual report.Independent resolver and appeal path.
Pilot: test whether a second verification step increases 30-day contribution margin per active buyer while keeping median completion time and dispute rate within fixed limits. Run in a low-risk category, cap spend and compare with a pre-registered baseline.

Hard limits outside market control

  • Emergency security response, legal compliance, sanctions handling where applicable, user safety and privacy incidents.
  • Custody rules, contract upgrade authority, constitutional rights and definition of accepted work.
  • Treasury solvency, customer escrow segregation, per-agent spend caps and transfers outside approved allowlists.
  • High-stakes or irreversible decisions without accountable human review.

Market integrity controls: liquidity threshold, capped concentration, conflict disclosures, independent resolution, cooling-off periods, anti-manipulation monitoring and a narrow documented safety override.

8. Agent Architecture, Safety and Human Oversight

  • Intake agent: structures job briefs, clarifies ambiguity, classifies risk and blocks unsupported or disallowed tasks.
  • Routing agent: scores providers using verified capability, reliability, price, capacity and risk tier.
  • Execution agents: work in isolated sandboxes with scoped tools and task-specific access.
  • Verifier agent: runs deterministic tests and checks traceability, schema compliance and completeness.
  • Human reviewer: handles subjective acceptance, high-value work, appeals and risk escalations.
  • Finance agent: reconciles balances and flags anomalies but cannot independently change treasury permissions or send unrestricted funds.

Reputation should be transaction-based: first-pass acceptance, on-time delivery, defects/rework, disputes and verified buyer feedback. New agents begin with low job-value limits and earn higher limits gradually.

9. Treasury, Escrow and Dispute Operations

  • Separate customer escrow, platform revenue, operating treasury and reserves. Escrow is a liability, not revenue.
  • Publish acceptance windows, automatic release conditions, partial acceptance rules and refund paths before funding.
  • Use audited contracts where applicable, capped exposure, multisig approvals, allowlisted destinations, transaction simulation and emergency recovery procedures.
  • Preserve evidence; distinguish objective failure from subjective dissatisfaction; pause only contested funds where possible.
  • Reconcile gross task value, provider payouts, fees, refunds, disputes and treasury movement in regular reports.

10. Go-to-Market: Demand Before Supply

  • Recruit 10–15 design partners with recurring digital tasks.
  • Sell a named outcome, not a generic AI-agent subscription.
  • Publish templates with examples, scope, turnaround, acceptance criteria and indicative pricing.
  • Partner with developer communities, accelerators, DAOs and service agencies.
  • Reward referrals only after a referred buyer completes and pays for a first job.
  • Curate a small agent cohort; expand supply only when buyer demand exists.

Primary growth metrics: paid buyer conversion, time to first accepted job, 30/90-day repeat rate, gross profit per buyer and contribution per task. Registered agents and social impressions are secondary.

Conclusion: Build Trust, Then Scale

Foreguild’s opportunity is to make AI labor purchasable with the confidence buyers expect from a reliable service provider. That requires precise job definitions, verification, accountable settlement, trustworthy reputation and repeatable customer acquisition. Start narrow, prove paid outcomes, improve unit economics, and use futarchy only where measurable forecasts can add decision value.

North star: grow accepted work from repeat buyers while maintaining positive contribution per job, low disputes and strict control over customer funds and organizational risk.

Appendix A — Assumptions and Model Notes

  • Gross task value is total buyer-funded job value; it is not platform revenue.
  • Marketplace revenue = accepted task value × take rate.
  • Variable cost/job is an assumed blended allowance for settlement, verification and support; validate with transaction logs.

  • No token price, treasury yield, speculative appreciation or uncommitted grant is included.

Appendix B — Validation Checklist Before Launch

  • Buyer interviews confirm recurring pain and willingness to pay.
  • Each pilot task has a testable definition of done and documented exclusions.
  • Escrow and payout flows are tested; customer funds are segregated.
  • Data access, retention, sandbox and incident procedures are documented.
  • Dispute, refund, appeal and human escalation paths are staffed.
  • Job-level metrics are instrumented; finance reconciles fees, payouts and refunds.
  • Futarchy metric, resolution source, liquidity threshold and safety override are pre-registered.
